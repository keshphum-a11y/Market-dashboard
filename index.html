How to read this page

FII, DII, Client, Pro
FII is foreign institutions, DII is domestic institutions (mutual funds, insurers), Client is retail and other individual traders, Pro is proprietary trading desks of brokers and arbitrage firms.
Long, Short, Net
Long means contracts bought, a bet on a rise. Short means contracts sold, a bet on a fall. Net is long minus short. A positive net means the group is net long.
Index vs stock segments
Index futures and options track Nifty or Bank Nifty. Stock futures and options track single shares. This data is by category, not by stock name.
Open interest (OI)
Number of contracts still open. Rising OI with a rising price usually means fresh buying; rising OI with a falling price usually means fresh selling.
Put-call ratio (PCR)
Put OI divided by call OI. Above 1 is often read as bullish support, below 0.7 as bearish. Extreme readings can also signal crowded positioning.
Max pain
The strike where option buyers collectively lose the most. Prices often drift toward it near expiry.
OI walls
For the nearest expiry: the strike at or below spot with the largest put OI (a likely floor) and the strike at or above spot with the largest call OI (a likely ceiling).
SMA and EMA
Moving averages of closing price over N days. SMA is the plain average; EMA weights recent days more. Price above them suggests an uptrend.
India VIX and implied move
India VIX is the annualised volatility the options market expects. Spot times VIX times the square root of (days / 365) gives a one-standard-deviation expected move for the period; roughly two-thirds of outcomes fall inside it.
ATR
Average True Range over 14 days: the typical daily price swing in points. It shows volatility, not direction.
Option spreads
A spread combines a bought and a sold option so the maximum loss is capped. Examples: bull put spread, bear call spread, iron condor.
