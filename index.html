How to read this page

FII, DII, Client, Pro
FII is foreign institutions, DII is domestic institutions (mutual funds, insurers), Client is retail and other individual traders, Pro is proprietary trading desks of brokers and arbitrage firms.
Long, Short, Net
Long means contracts bought, a bet on a rise. Short means contracts sold, a bet on a fall. Net is long minus short. A positive net means the group is net long.
Index vs stock segments
Index futures and options track Nifty or Bank Nifty. Stock futures and options track single shares. This data is by category, not by stock name.
Open interest (OI)
Number of contracts still open. Rising OI with a rising price usually means fresh buying; rising OI with a falling price usually means fresh selling.
Put-call ratio (PCR)
Put OI divided by call OI. Above 1 is often read as bullish support, below 0.7 as bearish. Extreme readings can also signal crowded positioning.
Max pain
The strike where option buyers collectively lose the most. Prices often drift toward it near expiry.
OI walls
For the nearest expiry: the strike at or below spot with the largest put OI (a likely floor) and the strike at or above spot with the largest call OI (a likely ceiling).
SMA and EMA
Moving averages of closing price over N days. SMA is the plain average; EMA weights recent days more. Price above them suggests an uptrend.
India VIX and implied move
India VIX is the annualised volatility the options market expects. Spot times VIX times the square root of (days / 365) gives a one-standard-deviation expected move for the period; roughly two-thirds of outcomes fall inside it.
ATR
Average True Range over 14 days: the typical daily price swing in points. It shows volatility, not direction.
Option spreads
A spread combines a bought and a sold option so the maximum loss is capped. Examples: bull put spread, bear call spread, iron condor.

Best strategy and risk as an option writer

HeadingConclusion
Best pick if I were an option writerBear call spread: sell 26,000 call and buy 26,300 call. Optional: add a far put spread (sell 24,000 put, buy 23,700 put) to make it a bearish-skewed iron condor.
Why this oneTime decay earns the premium while Nifty stays inside the range; the sold strikes sit outside the one-standard-deviation zone (24,728 to 25,971) and behind the option walls (24,000 and 26,000), and the bought wings cap the loss on a gap. I choose defined risk because one bad gap erases many months of premium.
Premium environmentIndia VIX 17.7 is rich: selling pays well, but it is rich because risk is higher, so keep size smaller.
Strike selectionSell outside the one-standard-deviation range and behind the OI walls, roughly 15 to 20 delta; buy wings 300 points further out.
Entry timingEnter when India VIX is calm or falling, not on a high-impact event day (next: US PCE price index (Oct), Q3 GDP second estimate and durable goods, Wed 25 Nov). Decay is fastest in the last 2 to 3 sessions, but so is gamma risk.
Adjustments and exitsBook profit at 50 to 70% of the maximum; exit if Nifty closes beyond a short strike or the matching OI wall; roll the untested side closer only in a calm market; never average a losing short.
Capital and riskCap the maximum loss per trade at 1 to 2% of capital; check the broker margin calculator; keep margin use under about 30 to 40% of capital to survive a gap. Hedged structures need far less margin than naked ones.
Expiry-day ruleShort options carry the most gamma risk on expiry day; close or hedge shorts by early afternoon and do not hold naked shorts into the last hour.
What I would avoidNaked short straddles when VIX is low, selling calls into a strong uptrend without a hedge, selling inside the one-standard-deviation range without wings, and adding to a losing short.
