How to read this page

SMA and EMA
Moving averages of the closing price over N days. SMA is the plain average; EMA gives more weight to recent days so it reacts faster. Price above them suggests an uptrend, below suggests a downtrend. The 200-day is the classic long-term trend line.
RSI
A 0 to 100 gauge (14 days) of how fast price has risen or fallen. Above 70 is often called overbought, below 30 oversold, near 50 neutral.
MACD
The gap between the 12 and 26 day EMAs, with a 9 day signal line. MACD above the signal line means momentum is turning up; below means it is fading.
Bollinger Bands
A 20 day average with bands 2 standard deviations above and below. Price near the upper band is stretched up, near the lower band stretched down.
ATR
Typical daily price swing in points over 14 days. It shows how volatile the market is, not the direction.
Pivot, R1, S1
Support and resistance levels calculated from the previous high, low and close. Traders watch them as possible turning points.
Put-call ratio (PCR)
Total put open interest divided by total call open interest. Above 1 is often read as bullish, below 0.7 as bearish.
Max pain
The strike where option buyers collectively lose the most. Prices often drift toward it near expiry.
Open interest (OI)
Number of contracts still open. Big OI at a strike marks a level traders are defending.
India VIX
How much movement the market expects over the next 30 days. High means nervous, low means calm.
FII and DII
Foreign and domestic institutional investors. Net FII buying usually supports the market; net selling pressures it.
